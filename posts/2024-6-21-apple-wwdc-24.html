Apple WWDC 2024

Jun 20, 2024 7 min to read

I know I'm a little late to the party with this post, but I wanted to take adequate time to sit down and reflect on Apple's 2024 edition of the Worldwide Developers Conference.

For those of you who don't know what WWDC is, Apple holds an annual conference where they showcase new technologies in both hardware and software. This year was particularly critical for Apple, as they announced several groundbreaking (dare I say innovative) markers as to what the future of Apple will look like. I would like to share some stuff that stood out to me as well as some general thoughts on the conference.


AI is Here

While the topic of artificial intelligence was not brought up for quite some time in the keynote address, I believe it was the most important part of the event as it empowered Apple's stock price to reach an all-time high in the following days. AI has already been around in some smaller aspects of Apple devices for quite some time, but LLM's and generative models have not been heavily integrated and apparent in any device to date. This changed in WWDC 2024.


Apple Intelligence introduces some new features that will be integrated into (only the highest-end versions) of Apple devices. One example provided in the address was writing tools. Writing tools can summarize, rewrite, or proofread all sorts of different texts. Apple will also follow suit with some AI photo tools that have been around in Android phones for quite some time. More on that later.


Generative AI will now be used in Siri as well. I am personally most excited about this. I am a big fan of the voice assistant but have found myself abstaining from using Siri in the past few years because, let's face it, she's bad. Apple will also be allowing users to type to Siri instead of just speaking. I think this is long overdue, but I'm glad we're finally getting this feature.


As I said before, only the highest-end versions will support some of these new features. Specifically, any iPhone 15 Pro and any iPad or Mac with Apple M1 silicon or later will be the cutoff for devices equipped with this new technology.


Naturally, there have been some privacy concerns with the new wave of AI in Apple devices. Elon Musk was quick to criticize Apple for privacy practices via posts on X. He even threatened to ban Apple devices from all of his companies. The main concern is differentiating what aspects of AI happen on the device and what aspects happen in the cloud. This concern was perpetuated by the fact that Apple announced a partnership with OpenAI to integrate ChatGPT into Apple AI. OpenAI's privacy practices have been under fire for quite some time by the public, so when this partnership was announced, Apple felt some of this heat.


YouTuber MKBHD asked Apple about the specifics of what happens on device vs. the cloud. He said nearly everything happens on device, but if there are things that are too complex for Apple's model, it will get sent to a larger, server-based model for private computation. In this server-based model, information is never stored or sent to Apple. On the other hand, if Apple Intelligence is faced with a problem that ChatGPT would be good at, it will specifically ask you if you would like to use ChatGPT for computation. After that, you the user can say yes or no to this prompt. OpenAI is also never allowed to store any of these requests, and Apple has stated that they will obscure your IP address for requests to ChatGPT. From my perspective, it seems like Apple is doing a good job making sure that this leap into AI is done with serious concern and care for privacy


iOS 18 - A Massive Update

iOS 18 is one of the biggest updates to the iPhone in quite some time. We finally see several features that Android users have had for years make their way to the iPhone.

Here are some of the features. I am not going to explain them in great detail. If you want to learn more about what they are, you can probably find a pretty good deal on a Samsung Galaxy from 2018 and test them yourself. Seriously, Android have had these features for a long time...


  • Put icons wherever you want on the home screen
  • Home screen themes
  • Customizable control center
  • Scheduled text messages
  • Photo app updates
  • And more!

As I touched on earlier, Apple will include photo cleanup tools as a part of Apple Intelligence. Deleting backgrounds, moving subjects, and more. Yes, Android has had this for years.


Vision Pro

Remember the craze with Vision Pro? Well, there are some small, but cool, new features that might help bring that hype back to life.


Apple added some new hand gestures to add some functionality while using the Vision Pro. Mac mirroring also got a lot better, and you can now double the resolution of your Mac screen with Vision Pro allowing the mirror to be a lot bigger without sacrificing resolution. There are many other minor updates, so it will be interesting to see how Apple keeps building on the Vision Pro.


iPad

While lots expected WWDC 2024 to be used by Apple as a big showcase for new iPad features, many were underwhelmed. The iPad finally got the calculator app (woohoo! only like 15 years later!). But seriously, there some cool features with it. The calculator app also came with what is called "Math Notes." This allows a user to write down equations (in handwriting) and will spit out an answer (in your handwriting). I thought this feature was really interesting. It also updates the answer if you make changes to your writing. Variables are also supported, meaning it can get information from everywhere on the page. When you update one of the variables, it will propagate through to wherever else you use it on the page.

We also got remote control so you can help family members with issues they might be having on their iPad. I thought this was a cool feature that is also considerate for older iPad users.


MacOS

Sequoia was announced as the latest version of MacOS. Some of the new features are actually pretty cool, some overdue. Window snapping is now built in. Backgrounds are built in. There is a new password app that syncs accross devices. iPhone mirroring was also introduced which will allow the user wirelessly see your iPhone on your Mac. You can drag and drop items back and forth from your Mac to iPhone and vice versa.

I will still probably be using PC for the forseeable future, but for Mac users, there you gso.


Final Thoughts

Overall, I'd say I have mixed feelings about Apple's WWDC 2024. While there are some features are very cool and I am excited for, there are others that I question. Firstly, I am a big fan of Apple leaning in to AI, and it seems like other investors are too. Apple stock shot up in days after the announcement. It seems like AI goes hand-in-hand with growth in our day and age, and it seems feels like a requirement for modern tech companies.


The part I really wasn't a fan of was the announcement of iOS. Specifically, themes. The ability to change the colors of all app icons seems like something that goes against Apple's identity. Typically, Apple likes everything to look neat and clean on their devices. On X, people have come up with some concept themes that look HORRIBLE. I enjoy the freedom of being able to customize my home screen, but this seems like something Steve Jobs would have never approved. Who knows, maybe it will grow on me. But for now, this was the thing that stood out to me as the worst new feature. Like I said, I just don't see how it fits with Apple's brand identity.


Aside from that, I really like all of the little features across all of the other devices. They seem like nice touches that I could see myself using.


As for stock implications, it's hard to not see this WWDC as great. I believe this sentiment was echoed throughout the markets the last week or so. Leaning into this new sector creates huge growth potential. It's hard to not give Apple the buy signal even as it sits near its all-time high. I will be updating my model soon, so stay tuned for a new price target.
