Apple WWDC 2024

I know I'm a little late to the party with this post, but I wanted to take adequate time to sit down and reflect on Apple's 2024 edition of the Worldwide Developers Conference.

For those of you who don't know what WWDC is, Apple holds an annual conference where they showcase new technologies in both hardware and software. This year was particularly critical for Apple, as they announced several groundbreaking (dare I say innovative) markers as to what the future of Apple will look like.

AI is Here

While the topic of artificial intelligence was not brought up for quite some time in the keynote address, I believe it was the most important part of the event as it empowered Apple's stock price to reach an all-time high in the following days.

Apple Intelligence introduces some new features that will be integrated into (only the highest-end versions) of Apple devices. Writing tools can summarize, rewrite, or proofread all sorts of different texts. Generative AI will now be used in Siri as well. I am personally most excited about this. Apple will also be allowing users to type to Siri instead of just speaking.

Naturally, there have been some privacy concerns. YouTuber MKBHD asked Apple about the specifics of what happens on device vs. the cloud. He said nearly everything happens on device, but if there are things that are too complex for Apple's model, it will get sent to a larger, server-based model for private computation. In this server-based model, information is never stored or sent to Apple.

iOS 18 - A Massive Update

iOS 18 is one of the biggest updates to the iPhone in quite some time. We finally see several features that Android users have had for years make their way to the iPhone:

  • Put icons wherever you want on the home screen
  • Home screen themes
  • Customizable control center
  • Scheduled text messages
  • Photo app updates

Vision Pro, iPad, and MacOS

Apple added some new hand gestures for Vision Pro and improved Mac mirroring. The iPad finally got a calculator app—complete with "Math Notes," which allows you to write equations by hand and see results in your own handwriting. MacOS Sequoia was also announced, featuring window snapping, a new password app, and iPhone mirroring that lets you drag and drop items wirelessly between devices.

Final Thoughts

Overall, I'd say I have mixed feelings. I'm a big fan of Apple leaning into AI, but I'm not a fan of the new home screen themes. The ability to change the colors of all app icons seems like something that goes against Apple's identity. However, from a stock perspective, it's hard not to see this WWDC as great. Leaning into this sector creates huge growth potential.
